\begin{abstract}
Resource counts in dimension-bounded self-testing depend on both the unknown
measurement and the reference preparations. We consider a complex-qubit
prepare-and-measure experiment with exactly four preparations, binary auxiliary
settings, and an extremal four-outcome target positive operator-valued measure.
The reference preparation associated with each target outcome is its pure kernel
state. We prove that every real linear witness on the complete probability table
that is globally maximized at this reference admits an inequivalent maximizing
target with at most three nonzero effects whenever the auxiliary observable
Bloch vectors span at most two dimensions. The proof first uses measurement
optimization and complementary slackness to force all target coefficients into
a common-row-shift plus nonnegative exclusion-penalty form. Preparation optimality
then leaves at most two inputs with nonzero auxiliary contributions, allowing an
explicit positive and complete replacement measurement. The result includes biased
and degenerate binary measurements and allows input-independent shared randomness.
It obstructs reducing auxiliary resources within the matched-kernel reference
class used by existing constructions. It does not establish unrestricted resource
minimality, a bound on preparation number, or robustness to noise.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum self-testing; qubit measurements;
prepare-and-measure scenarios; resource bounds; semidefinite duality.

\section{Introduction}

Dimension-bounded prepare-and-measure self-testing certifies an unknown quantum
realization from observed probabilities and a bound on the transmitted Hilbert
space. For a nonprojective qubit measurement, the number of preparation inputs
and auxiliary measurement settings is an experimentally relevant resource.
Tavakoli \emph{et al.}~\cite{Tavakoli2020} developed a construction that first
certifies suitable preparations and then adds penalties for target outcomes
excluded by those states. Dr\'otos \emph{et al.}~\cite{Drotos2024} obtained
four-preparation, three-binary-auxiliary constructions for arbitrary extremal
four-outcome qubit measurements and conjectured their resource minimality.
Their preparation-only optimization places optimal state directions in the
auxiliary Bloch span. By itself, that observation does not constrain a general
witness whose target probabilities also enter preparation optimization.

Here we address this distinction within the matched state-exclusion reference
class. All sixteen target coefficients are initially arbitrary, and the witness
need only certify the target. Measurement optimality nevertheless forces the
target contribution to be a constant plus nonnegative exclusion penalties.
We then construct an inequivalent target attaining the same global score when
the auxiliary Bloch span has dimension at most two. Thus changing the weights
of the complete probability table cannot reduce the auxiliary count at these
references.

This restriction is the one used in the cited constructions, rather than an
assumption about all possible self-tests. The revised semi-SIC
construction~\cite{DrotosSemiSIC} also uses matched exclusion states; its
discussion asks about smaller setups. General preparation and measurement
self-testing constructions~\cite{Navascues2023} establish existence with other
auxiliary resources. A recent photonic semi-SIC realization~\cite{Xu2026}
uses four preparations, three dichotomic settings and the target.
Our result supplies a necessary resource condition for matched references;
the unrestricted minimality question is not resolved here.

\section{Model and reference strategies}
\label{sec:model}

Alice receives an input $i\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ and sends a single complex qubit to
Bob. Bob chooses either a binary auxiliary setting $y\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$ or
the target setting $t$, which has four labeled outcomes. A deterministic
strategy consists of density operators $\rho_i\geq0$, $\tr\rho_i=1$,
binary effects $B_{b|y}\geq0$, $B_{0|y}+B_{1|y}=\id$, and target effects
$E_j\geq0$, $\sum_{j=1}^4E_j=\id$. The probabilities are
\begin{equation}
 p(b|i,y)=\tr(\rho_i B_{b|y}),\qquad
 p(j|i,t)=\tr(\rho_i E_j).
 \label{eq:born}
\end{equation}
The competing target may have zero effects or be nonextremal. No entanglement
assistance, trusted axes, sequential instrument or additional communication is
allowed. Input-independent shared randomness permits convex mixtures of these
strategies. All quantities are dimensionless, and the problem is static and
exactly noiseless.

Use the Pauli convention $(\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z)$, with
$(\sigma_y)_{01}=-\mathrm{i}$. The reference is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_i^\star=\frac{\id+\rv_i\cdot\bs}{2},\qquad
 T_i=\alpha_i(\id-\rv_i\cdot\bs),
 \label{eq:reference}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_i>0,\quad \norm{\rv_i}=1,\quad
 \sum_i\alpha_i=1,\quad \sum_i\alpha_i\rv_i=0.
 \label{eq:complete}
\end{equation}
The four $T_i$ are linearly independent Hermitian matrices, as required for
an extremal four-outcome qubit measurement. In particular,
$\tr(\rho_i^\star T_i)=0$: each preparation is the pure state supported on
the kernel of its matched effect. For the reference binary observables write
\begin{equation}
 O_y:=B_{0|y}-B_{1|y}=\beta_y\id+\dv_y\cdot\bs,
 \qquad |\beta_y|+\norm{\dv_y}\leq1.
 \label{eq:binary}
\end{equation}
The inequality is equivalent to positivity of both binary effects; it includes
projective, biased, unsharp and degenerate measurements.

Binary normalization puts every real linear witness on the complete table into
the form
\begin{equation}
 L=C_0+\sum_{i,y}w_{iy}\tr(\rho_iO_y)
       +\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}\tr(\rho_iE_j),
 \label{eq:witness}
\end{equation}
with arbitrary real $w_{iy},c_{ij}$. We say that $L$ self-tests the reference
target if every globally maximizing admissible realization has target effects
related to $T_i$ by one common unitary or antiunitary transformation. The input
and outcome labels are fixed. The obstruction below would remain valid even
if target outcome permutations were allowed, since it changes the number of
nonzero effects. No separate preparation self-test is assumed.

\section{Supporting witnesses and the resource obstruction}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:main}
Let the reference satisfy Eqs.~\eqref{eq:reference}--\eqref{eq:binary}, with
four linearly independent target effects. Suppose
$\dim\operatorname{span}\{\dv_y\}_{y=1}^K\leq2$.
If a real linear witness on the complete probability table attains its global
maximum at this reference, it also has an admissible maximizing realization
with the same auxiliary measurements and a target with at most three nonzero
effects. Consequently it cannot self-test the reference four-outcome target.
This includes any two arbitrary binary auxiliary measurements and allows
input-independent shared randomness.
\end{proposition}

We prove the proposition through a necessary coefficient form and an explicit
replacement strategy.

\subsection{A basis property of the reference}

The four $\rho_i^\star$ form a basis of the real vector space of Hermitian
$2\times2$ matrices. Indeed, independence of the $T_i$ is independence of
the coefficient vectors $(1,-\rv_i)$, which is unchanged by reversing the
three Pauli coordinates. Furthermore, every triple of $\rv_i$ is linearly
independent. If one triple spanned at most a plane through the origin,
the positive-weight relation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:complete} would place the
fourth direction in the same plane. All four effects would then belong to
a Hermitian subspace of dimension at most three, a contradiction.
Thus no plane through the origin contains more than two reference directions,
and no two are collinear.

\subsection{The target coefficient normal form}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:normal}
If the reference maximizes $L$, there exist real numbers $\ell_i$ and
nonnegative numbers $k_j$ such that
\begin{equation}
 c_{ij}=\ell_i-k_j\delta_{ij}.
 \label{eq:normal}
\end{equation}
For every admissible strategy, its target contribution is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i,j}c_{ij}p(j|i,t)=\sum_i\ell_i-\sum_i k_i p(i|i,t).
 \label{eq:penalty}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Hold the reference preparations and auxiliaries fixed. Global optimality
implies that the reference target solves the measurement semidefinite program
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \text{maximize}\quad &\sum_j\tr(H_jE_j),
 & H_j&=\sum_i c_{ij}\rho_i^\star,\\
 \text{subject to}\quad &E_j\geq0,
 & \sum_jE_j&=\id.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:primal}
\end{equation}
Its dual minimizes $\tr\Lambda$ over Hermitian $\Lambda$ with
$\Lambda-H_j\geq0$ for each $j$. This follows directly from the Lagrangian
$\tr\Lambda+\sum_j\tr[(H_j-\Lambda)E_j]$.
The primal is strictly feasible at $E_j=\id/4$ and has a compact feasible
set. The dual is strictly feasible at $\Lambda=t\id$ for sufficiently
large $t$. Finite-dimensional Slater duality~\cite{Boyd2004} therefore
provides an attained dual optimum and complementary slackness:
\begin{equation}
 S_j:=\Lambda-H_j\geq0,\qquad \tr(S_jT_j)=0.
 \label{eq:slack}
\end{equation}
For positive operators, zero trace of the product implies orthogonal supports.
Since $T_j$ has rank one on a two-dimensional space, its complementary
support is the one-dimensional support of $\rho_j^\star$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 S_j=k_j\rho_j^\star,\qquad k_j\geq0.
 \label{eq:rankone}
\end{equation}
This includes zero slack. Expand the common dual operator uniquely as
$\Lambda=\sum_i\ell_i\rho_i^\star$. Comparing coefficients in the state
basis gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal}.

This equality constrains the numerical coefficients of the original witness,
rather than merely its value at the reference. It consequently remains true
when preparations and measurements change. Summing each target probability
row to one gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:penalty}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Only two preparations can contribute to auxiliary optimization}

Keep all reference measurements fixed and define
\begin{equation}
 \uv_i=\sum_yw_{iy}\dv_y.
 \label{eq:u}
\end{equation}
For a varied preparation $\rho_i=(\id+\mathbf{s}\cdot\bs)/2$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:penalty} makes the state-dependent witness term
\begin{equation}
 (\uv_i+k_i\alpha_i\rv_i)\cdot\mathbf{s},
 \qquad \norm{\mathbf{s}}\leq1.
 \label{eq:gradient}
\end{equation}
Because $\rv_i$ is globally optimal with these measurements fixed,
\begin{equation}
 \uv_i+k_i\alpha_i\rv_i=\tau_i\rv_i,
 \qquad \tau_i\geq0.
 \label{eq:alignment}
\end{equation}
The case $\tau_i=0$ allows a completely vanishing preparation gradient.
In particular $\uv_i=(\tau_i-k_i\alpha_i)\rv_i$; its multiplier may have
either sign. Every nonzero $\uv_i$ is parallel to a reference direction
and belongs to the auxiliary span. The basis property above and the span
hypothesis imply
\begin{equation}
 A:=\{i:\uv_i\ne0\},\qquad |A|\leq2.
 \label{eq:active}
\end{equation}
For an inactive label, the auxiliary contribution is independent of the
preparation. Individual $w_{iy}$ need not vanish: dependent or degenerate
auxiliary directions can cancel. The constants $\sum_yw_{iy}\beta_y$
are also preserved if the auxiliaries are unchanged.

\subsection{A positive complete target with the same score}

Suppose first that $A=\{a,b\}$. Let $c,d$ be the two inactive labels, and set
\begin{equation}
 s=\norm{\rv_a+\rv_b},\qquad
 \qv=\frac{\rv_a+\rv_b}{s},\qquad a_0=\frac{1}{2+s}.
 \label{eq:q}
\end{equation}
Noncollinearity gives $0<s<2$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 E'_a&=a_0(\id-\rv_a\cdot\bs),&
 E'_b&=a_0(\id-\rv_b\cdot\bs),\\
 E'_c&=a_0s(\id+\qv\cdot\bs),& E'_d&=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:replacement}
\end{equation}
Each effect is positive. Their scalar coefficients sum to $a_0(2+s)=1$,
and their Pauli coefficients sum to
$-a_0(\rv_a+\rv_b)+a_0s\qv=0$. Thus they sum to $\id$.
Retain $\rho_a^\star,\rho_b^\star$, take
$\rho'_c=(\id-\qv\cdot\bs)/2$, and choose any density operator for $d$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \tr(\rho'_iE'_i)=0\quad\text{for every }i.
 \label{eq:newzeros}
\end{equation}

If $A=\{a\}$, choose an inactive $c$ and use instead
\begin{equation}
 E'_a=\frac{\id-\rv_a\cdot\bs}{2},\qquad
 E'_c=\frac{\id+\rv_a\cdot\bs}{2},
 \label{eq:projective}
\end{equation}
with the other two effects zero, $\rho'_a=\rho_a^\star$ and
$\rho'_c=(\id-\rv_a\cdot\bs)/2$. The remaining preparations are arbitrary.
When $A$ is empty, the same projective construction uses any two distinct
labels and any unit vector in place of $\rv_a$. These targets are positive
and complete, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:newzeros} holds in both cases.

Keep all auxiliary effects fixed. Active preparation contributions are
unchanged, and inactive auxiliary contributions remain state-independent.
All scalar biases and row-shift constants are unchanged, while every target
penalty remains zero. Therefore $L'=L^\star$. Since the reference already
attains the global maximum, the replacement does as well. It has at least
one zero effect, so it is inequivalent to four nonzero reference effects
under common unitary or antiunitary transformations, including any outcome
permutation. A single deterministic alternative suffices to prevent
self-testing. Allowing shared randomness cannot eliminate that alternative;
a linear score has the same maximum over strategies and their convex hull.
This proves Proposition~\ref{prop:main}. \qed

\section{Consequences and limitations}

For exactly four matched pure kernel preparations, target self-testing by a
globally optimal linear witness requires an auxiliary Bloch span of dimension
three. At least three binary settings are therefore necessary in this reference
class, although three settings need not suffice for arbitrary choices of the
auxiliaries. The statement covers the entire coefficient array of the complete
table and does not require independent certification of the preparations.
The supporting coefficient form, followed by a physically admissible competing
maximizer, extends the preparation-only span obstruction.

The replacement preserves the witness score, not the full probability table.
In particular, no operational equivalence of the two realizations is asserted
for measurements or statistics that are not evaluated through the chosen
linear score. The matched-kernel hypothesis is used both in the rank-one dual
slack and in preparation alignment. Self-tests with other reference preparations
lie outside the result. Additional preparation inputs, nonlinear criteria,
entanglement-assisted or sequential models, necessity of four preparations,
and a noise-robust tradeoff are also outside its scope. The broader conjecture
about resource minimality~\cite{Drotos2024} consequently remains unresolved by
this argument.

The derivation is exact, without a perturbative order or numerical tolerance.
The accompanying symbolic checks reconstruct the full complementary-slackness
solution space and verify positivity, completeness, diagonal exclusions and
score equality for all eleven possible active-label sets on tetrahedral and
unequal-weight extremal references. These finite checks support the algebra;
the universal quantifiers follow from the analytic proof. No finite set of
examples is used to infer the general obstruction.
