\title{Exact Tsirelson bound for a three-output Bell functional}

\author{Xiaojian Zeng}
\email{xiaojianzeng@foxmail.com}
\affiliation{PBC School of Finance, Tsinghua University, Beijing 100190, China}

\date{September 15, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We determine the exact quantum value of the three-output, two-versus-three-input
Bell functional that forms the $d=3$ member of the first family introduced by
Perito \textit{et al.}  The defining work exhibited a maximally entangled
qutrit strategy with value $4$ and found a matching upper bound at the
$1+AB$ level of the Navascu\'es--Pironio--Ac\'in hierarchy to numerical
precision.  Here we give a finite exact certificate over the cyclotomic field
$\Q(\omega)$, $\omega^3=1$, $\omega\ne1$.  A $35\times35$ Hermitian Gram
matrix has exact rank $16$, is positive semidefinite by a rational
pivoted-$LDL^\dagger$ factorization, and expands to $4\,\id-I_3$ using only the
order-three measurement relations and Alice--Bob commutation.  Consequently
$I_3\leq4$ in arbitrary local dimension, while the qutrit construction proves
attainability.  The result fixes the Tsirelson value but does not classify
equality strategies or by itself imply self-testing, robustness, or
device-independent randomness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Bell inequalities turn restrictions on quantum correlations into quantitative
operator bounds.  For inequalities with several inputs and outputs, sharp
quantum values are often first identified through semidefinite relaxations of
the quantum moment problem, notably the Navascu\'es--Pironio--Ac\'in (NPA)
hierarchy~\cite{NPA2007,NPA2008,Doherty2008}.  A numerical optimum, however,
does not by itself give an exact dimension-independent operator inequality.
When a finite NPA dual can be reconstructed as a noncommutative
sum of Hermitian squares (SOS), the numerical observation can instead be
replaced by an algebraic certificate~\cite{Helton2002}.

Perito \textit{et al.} recently introduced two families of multi-output Bell
functionals motivated by optimal global-randomness certification
\cite{Perito2026}.  For their first family, they conjectured an exact quantum
value in every output dimension.  At $d=3$, the conjectured value is $4$.
Their work supplies an explicit value-$4$ qutrit strategy and a $1+AB$ NPA
upper bound agreeing with $4$ to within $10^{-6}$, whereas the available
analytic estimate is $3\sqrt{2}$~\cite{Perito2026}.  The exact SOS and
self-testing arguments in that work concern its distinct second family.

In this paper we close the value gap for the $d=3$ member of the first family.
The central object is a machine-readable Gram matrix over $\Q(\omega)$ on the
complete $35$-word Fourier-unitary $1+AB$ span.  Both the coefficient identity
and positive semidefiniteness are checked with exact rational arithmetic.  The
certificate proves the upper bound without a local-dimension cutoff.  We keep
the stronger equality problem separate: neither the certificate rank nor its
kernel alone establishes rigidity or randomness.

\section{Bell scenario and convention}

Alice chooses $x\in\{0,1\}$, Bob chooses $y\in\{0,1,2\}$, and both outcomes
belong to $\mathbb Z_3$.  For three-output projective measurements
$\{M^A_{a|x}\}_{a=0}^2$ and $\{M^B_{b|y}\}_{b=0}^2$, define the Fourier
observables
\begin{equation}
 A_x=\sum_{a=0}^2\omega^a M^A_{a|x},\qquad
 B_y=\sum_{b=0}^2\omega^b M^B_{b|y},
 \label{eq:fourier}
\end{equation}
where $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$.  They obey
\begin{equation}
 A_x^3=B_y^3=\id,\qquad A_x^\dagger=A_x^2,
 \qquad B_y^\dagger=B_y^2.
 \label{eq:relations}
\end{equation}
Operators assigned to different parties commute; no relation is imposed
between distinct inputs of the same party.

We use the no-dagger convention of Eq.~(6) of Ref.~\cite{Perito2026}:
\begin{align}
 I_3=\frac12\bigg[&A_0\otimes(B_0+B_1+B_2) \nonumber\\
 &+A_1\otimes(B_0+\omega B_1+\omega^2B_2)
 +\mathrm{h.c.}\bigg].
 \label{eq:I3}
\end{align}
Appendix A of that reference writes $B_y^\dagger$ instead.  The two conventions
are equivalent only after recording the Bob outcome inversion
\begin{equation}
 M^B_{b|y}\longmapsto M^B_{-b\,\mathrm{mod}\,3|y},
 \qquad B_y\longmapsto B_y^\dagger.
 \label{eq:outcome-inversion}
\end{equation}
All statements below refer to Eq.~\eqref{eq:I3}.

\section{Exact cyclotomic Gram certificate}

Let $\mathcal A$ be the unital $*$-algebra generated by
$A_0,A_1,B_0,B_1,B_2$, quotiented by Eq.~\eqref{eq:relations} and by
$[A_x,B_y]=0$.  Define the ordered word column
\begin{equation}
 W=\left(\id,\ \{A_x^k\}_{x,k},\ \{B_y^\ell\}_{y,\ell},\
 \ \{A_x^kB_y^\ell\}_{x,k,y,\ell}\right)^{\mathsf T},
 \label{eq:words}
\end{equation}
where $x=0,1$, $y=0,1,2$, and $k,\ell=1,2$, with indices in lexicographic
order.  Thus $W$ contains $1+4+6+24=35$ words.

The supplemental certificate gives a Hermitian matrix
$Y\in M_{35}[\Q(\omega)]$.  An entry is stored as a rational pair $[a,b]$
representing $a+b\omega$; the overline below denotes entrywise complex
conjugation.  Its Gram convention is
\begin{equation}
 4\,\id-I_3
 =\sum_{i,j=1}^{35}\overline{Y_{ij}}\,w_i^\dagger w_j
 =W^\dagger\overline{Y}\,W .
 \label{eq:gram-identity}
\end{equation}
Complex conjugation does not change positive semidefiniteness, so it is enough
to certify $Y\succeq0$.

Two exact checks establish Eq.~\eqref{eq:gram-identity}.  First, every
$w_i^\dagger w_j$ is reduced by moving Alice words past Bob words and combining
adjacent powers of the same setting modulo $3$.  Collecting coefficients gives
exactly the constant $4$, the six terms of $-I_3/2$ displayed in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:I3}, and their adjoints; all other reduced-word coefficients
vanish.  Second, simultaneous exact row and column pivoting produces
\begin{equation}
 PYP^\dagger=L\,\mathrm{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_{16},0_{19})L^\dagger,
 \qquad d_r\in\Q_{>0},
 \label{eq:ldl}
\end{equation}
with all $d_r$ stored explicitly as positive rational numbers.  The trailing
$19\times19$ Schur complement is identically zero, not merely numerically
small.  Equation~\eqref{eq:ldl} proves $Y\succeq0$ and rank$(Y)=16$.
The exact field operations and replay procedure are summarized in the
Appendix.

It follows that $\overline{Y}=R^\dagger R$ for a $16\times35$ matrix $R$ over
the algebraic closure, and hence
\begin{equation}
 4\,\id-I_3=\sum_{r=1}^{16}P_r^\dagger P_r,
 \qquad P_r=\sum_i R_{ri}w_i .
 \label{eq:sos}
\end{equation}
This immediately gives the upper bound in every representation of the frozen
relations.

\section{Attainability and exact quantum value}

For completeness, consider the normalized maximally entangled qutrit state
\begin{equation}
 |\Phi_3\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt3}\sum_{k=0}^2|kk\rangle,
 \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
and generalized Pauli operators
\begin{equation}
 Z=\sum_{k=0}^2\omega^k|k\rangle\langle k|,
 \qquad X=\sum_{k=0}^2|k+1\rangle\langle k|.
\end{equation}
Take $A_0=Z$, $A_1=X$, and~\cite{Perito2026}
\begin{equation}
 B_y=\frac13\left(2Z^2+2\omega^{2y}X-\omega^{y+1}X^2Z\right).
 \label{eq:By}
\end{equation}
Direct multiplication in $\Q(\omega)$ gives
$B_y^\dagger B_y=B_y^3=\id$, so the inverse Fourier transform of each $B_y$
is a valid three-projector measurement.  The same arithmetic gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{y=0}^2B_y=2Z^2,\qquad
 \sum_{y=0}^2\omega^yB_y=2X.
 \label{eq:fourier-sums}
\end{equation}
Because $|\Phi_3\rangle$ is stabilized by $Z\otimes Z^2$ and $X\otimes X$,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:I3} and \eqref{eq:fourier-sums} yield
\begin{equation}
 \langle\Phi_3|I_3|\Phi_3\rangle=4.
 \label{eq:lower}
\end{equation}

Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:sos} and \eqref{eq:lower} proves the main result.

\textit{Theorem.}---For every tensor-product quantum strategy on arbitrary
local Hilbert spaces with the three-output projective measurements of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier},
\begin{equation}
 \Tr(\rho I_3)\leq4.
\end{equation}
The bound is attained by the two-qutrit strategy
\eqref{eq:phi}--\eqref{eq:By}; therefore the Tsirelson value of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:I3} is exactly $4$.

The operator identity actually uses only cross-party commutation, so the same
upper bound also holds for commuting-operator representations of these
relations.  Attainability is already finite dimensional.

\section{Comparison and limitations}

The certificate lies on the same $1+AB$ word span that produced the published
numerical upper bound~\cite{Perito2026}, but its logical status is different.
The matrix entries, coefficient comparison, and $LDL^\dagger$ pivots are all
exact.  Floating-point semidefinite optimization was used only to locate a
rational point in the affine Gram family and is not used in accepting the
identity or positivity.  The resulting inequality therefore has no numerical
error bar and no local-dimension truncation.

This conclusion is deliberately narrower than global-randomness certification.
If a strategy saturates the bound, the sixteen polynomials in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sos} annihilate the state on its support.  Turning those kernel
conditions into a classification up to local isometries would require a
separate rigidity argument.  The $19$-dimensional null space of the Gram
matrix is not such a classification.  We consequently make no claim about
self-testing, robustness, uniqueness of the behavior, or the guessing
probability of the extended functional.  Numerical robustness calculations
for the surrounding randomness protocol address a complementary question
\cite{DAvino2026}.

Nor does the present certificate establish the conjectured first-family value
for $d>3$.  Its field, word list, and symmetry reduction are specialized to
$\omega^3=1$.  Generalizing it requires a new dimension-dependent construction
rather than a relabeling of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gram-identity}.

\section{Conclusion}

We have converted the numerical value-$4$ observation for a three-output Bell
functional into a finite exact noncommutative SOS.  A rank-$16$ cyclotomic Gram
matrix proves $I_3\leq4$ in arbitrary dimension, and the known qutrit strategy
attains the bound.  This supplies a rigorous baseline for future analyses of
equality and randomness while leaving those stronger questions open.

\section{Exact replay of the certificate}

This appendix records the algebra used by the supplied verifier.  Elements of
$\Q(\omega)$ are represented by pairs $(a,b)\in\Q^2$ with
$a+b\omega$.  From $\omega^2=-1-\omega$,
\begin{align}
 (a,b)(c,d)&=(ac-bd,\ ad+bc-bd),\\
 \overline{(a,b)}&=(a-b,-b).
 \label{eq:field-arithmetic}
\end{align}
Thus every operation required by the proof reduces to integer and rational
arithmetic.

Each word is stored as an Alice sequence and a Bob sequence.  The reducer
combines adjacent occurrences of one setting modulo $3$ and deletes exponent
$0$.  Alice and Bob sequences are maintained separately, implementing only
their mutual commutation.  For each canonical reduced word $u$, the verifier
forms
\begin{equation}
 c(u)=\sum_{i,j:\,\operatorname{red}(w_i^\dagger w_j)=u}
 \overline{Y_{ij}}.
 \label{eq:coefficient-map}
\end{equation}
The target map has
\begin{align}
 c(\id)&=4,\\
 c(A_0B_y)=c(A_0^2B_y^2)&=-\tfrac12,\\
 c(A_1B_y)&=-\tfrac12\omega^y,\\
 c(A_1^2B_y^2)&=-\tfrac12\omega^{-y},
\end{align}
for $y=0,1,2$, and zero for every other word.  Exact dictionary equality
therefore verifies the full operator identity, rather than selected moments or
a trace.

For positivity, the verifier performs simultaneous row and column swaps, takes
a strictly positive rational diagonal pivot, and updates the remaining Schur
complement in $\Q(\omega)$.  After sixteen pivots, every entry in the remaining
$19\times19$ block is exactly $(0,0)$.  This is the factorization in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ldl}.  The machine-readable matrix, permutation, positive pivots,
and independent replay script are supplied with the manuscript.
