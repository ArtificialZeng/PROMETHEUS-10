# Exact continuum certificates for the first five new subdivided stars

## Result and scope

Let `T_q` have `q` ordinary leaves at a centre `C` and the two additional
edges `C--A--B`, and let

\[
 K_s=-H_s=\sum_e |\phi_s\rangle\langle\phi_s|_e,
 \qquad |\phi_s\rangle=|00\rangle+s|11\rangle,
 \qquad t=s^2.
\]

The exact computer-assisted certificate
`subdivided-star-continuum.json`, generated by
`certify_subdivided_star_continuum.py`, proves

\[
 \Delta(T_q,s)>1-s^2
 \quad\text{for every real }0<s<1
 \quad\text{and each }q=2,3,4,5,6.                 \tag{1}
\]

Together with the earlier analytic `P4=T_1` proof, this establishes (1)
for `1<=q<=6`, i.e. for this non-star family through nine vertices.  It is
a finite-range result, not a proof for arbitrary `q`, arbitrary trees, or
the all-connected-graph conjecture.

## Why the ground root is fixed

The conserved charge and leaf-spin decomposition from
`subdivided-star-analysis.md` gives blocks indexed by `(r,Q)`, each of
dimension at most eight.  Lemma 12 of the checked primary source makes the
largest eigenvalue of `K_s` simple for every `0<=s<1`.  At `s=0` it is the
all-zero state, which belongs to `(r,Q)=(0,0)`.  A simple eigenvalue cannot
move between invariant charge or permutation sectors without a degeneracy.
Consequently the physical top root remains the largest root of the
`(0,0)` factor throughout `0<=s<1`.  After its structural zero root is
removed, this factor has degree six.

The generator independently checks the spin-multiplicity dimension sum

\[
 \sum_r\left[\binom qr-\binom q{r-1}\right]
       \sum_Q \dim B_{q,r,Q}=2^{q+3},               \tag{2}
\]

so every physical competitor is represented.  Removing identically zero
roots loses no possible gap crossing: the all-zero trial state gives
`mu_e>=q+2`, hence `mu_e-0>1-t` for every case in (1).

## Exact crossing reduction

Write `P_q(x,t)` for the nonzero degree-six `(0,0)` factor and
`F_{q,r,Q}(x,t)` for any nonzero charge-spin factor, including `P_q`
itself when testing another root in the ground block.  If the actual gap
margin

\[
 g_q(t)=\mu_e(t)-\max\{\mu_o(t),\nu_e(t)\}-(1-t)   \tag{3}
\]

were zero at an interior parameter, then for some competitor `lambda`

\[
 P_q(\mu_e,t)=0,\qquad
 F_{q,r,Q}(\mu_e-(1-t),t)=0.
\]

Therefore that parameter must be a zero of the exact crossing resultant

\[
 R_{q,r,Q}(t)=\operatorname{Res}_x
 \bigl(P_q(x,t),F_{q,r,Q}(x-(1-t),t)\bigr).         \tag{4}
\]

All polynomials and resultants are constructed over `QQ[t,x]`; no fitted
coefficient or floating eigenvalue enters (4).  After explicitly removing
only powers of the endpoint factors `t` and `t-1`, exact real-root
isolation finds 171 interior candidate intervals across `q=2,...,6`
(duplicates between resultants are deliberately retained).  The packet
binds every primitive polynomial by a monomial-and-coefficient SHA-256
digest and records every rational isolating interval.

## Certification at every candidate root

At the rational midpoint `t_0` of every interval `[a,b]`, exact root
isolation of all charge-spin characteristic polynomials gives a rational
lower bound `m_0` on (3).  To extend that bound over the whole isolating
interval, note that in the `|00>,|11>` subspace

\[
 \left\|\frac{d}{ds}|\phi_s\rangle\langle\phi_s|\right\|
 =s+\sqrt{1+s^2}<\frac52
 \quad(0<s<1).
\]

There are `q+2` edges, so Weyl's inequality makes every ordered eigenvalue
`L_q`-Lipschitz in `s`, with

\[
 L_q=\frac52(q+2).
\]

The maximum over all competitors is also `L_q`-Lipschitz.  Since

\[
 |\sqrt t-\sqrt{t_0}|
 =\frac{|t-t_0|}{\sqrt t+\sqrt{t_0}}
 \le \frac{|t-t_0|}{2a},
\]

the variation of (3) anywhere in `[a,b]` is at most the saved rational
quantity

\[
 \frac{b-a}{2}\left(\frac{L_q}{a}+1\right).        \tag{5}
\]

For all 171 candidate intervals, the exact lower bound `m_0` is strictly
larger than (5).  The smallest resulting certified slack for each `q` is

| `q` | `n=q+3` | candidate intervals | minimum certified slack |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 5 | 18 | `1.962451766604367e-2` |
| 3 | 6 | 21 | `7.983488781184383e-3` |
| 4 | 7 | 33 | `4.107298454998542e-3` |
| 5 | 8 | 41 | `2.403722409619468e-3` |
| 6 | 9 | 58 | `1.047523079633299e-3` |

Finally, exact root isolation gives `g_q(1/4)>0` for every listed `q`.
If (3) vanished anywhere in `(0,1)`, (4) would put that point in one of
the certified positive neighborhoods.  This contradiction, together with
continuity, proves (1).

## Independent replay and limitations

`verify_subdivided_star_continuum.py` does not import the spin-reduction
generator.  It rebuilds both parity matrices in the full computational
basis, verifies all 171 saved rational enclosure inequalities, and checks
the reference point and least-slack neighborhood for every `q`.  The
direct replay agrees at all ten tested points; its floating values are
corroboration only, while the continuum conclusion rests on the exact
resultants, rational root isolation, and bound (5).

A salvage rerun in the same bound environment regenerated all five cases
with the same ground/resultant polynomial digests, 171 root counts, and exact
minimum slacks, and both the saved and regenerated packets passed the direct
checker. The JSON bytes differed because SymPy selected different valid
noncanonical rational isolating endpoints. Accordingly the evidence claims
semantic exact reproducibility, not byte-deterministic isolators; the actual
comparison is recorded in `salvage-validation.log`.

The certificate does not give a parameter-uniform symbolic sign table in
the unbounded integer `q`, and its cost grows because irrelevant
eigenvalue-pair crossings also appear in (4).  The next useful step is to
factor the large-`q` resultants by physical branch, or derive an
interlacing/Schur-complement comparison that removes those irrelevant
crossings before extending beyond `q=6` and attacking all `r>0` blocks
uniformly.
