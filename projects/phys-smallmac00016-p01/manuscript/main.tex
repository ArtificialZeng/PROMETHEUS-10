\begin{abstract}
Classical feedback from a measured environment is usually analysed with every Kraus
outcome resolved. We study two uses of a full-Choi-rank generalized-amplitude-damping
qubit instrument whose four outcomes are disclosed only through the bit $03|12$.
For $p=18/25$ and $\gamma=3/10$, arbitrary clock-dependent current-bit CPTP
recoveries have exact maximal entanglement fidelity $289/400$, attained by identity
recovery. A feasible controller retaining the first bit reaches $1831/2500$, an
advantage of $99/10000$ over the current-bit optimum. We also certify a rational
parameter rectangle on which the full-history advantage is uniformly at least
$1/1000$.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} environment-assisted correction; quantum feedback;
coarse-grained measurement; entanglement fidelity; generalized amplitude damping

\section{Introduction}

Measuring an environment converts a quantum noise process into a classical record
and a quantum instrument. When each record value resolves one Kraus operator,
conditional recovery can exploit its polar decomposition. Gregoratti and Werner
developed this discrete-time framework and obtained exact qubit recovery formulas for
resolved records~\cite{GregorattiWerner2004}. More recently, Rexiti, Khabbazi Oskouei,
and Mancini compared current-outcome and history-conditioned feedback in the same
resolved-record setting~\cite{RexitiEtAl2024}. Related Bayesian--Markovian distinctions
also occur in continuously monitored feedback, although there the dynamics and
objective are different~\cite{WisemanEtAl2002}.

A finite classical readout need not resolve the environment outcome. If several
orthogonal outcomes are assigned the same symbol, the disclosed branch is a
multi-Kraus completely positive operation: the hidden alternatives must be added
incoherently before recovery. Consequently, the controller cannot apply a separate
polar correction to each hidden Kraus operator. This information restriction is
distinct from optimizing a single recovery after fixed noise, which is a semidefinite
program~\cite{FletcherShorWin2007}, and from monotone alternating optimization of
encoding and decoding maps~\cite{ReimpellWerner2005}. The two-stage problem considered
below is bilinear even though either stage becomes convex when the other is fixed.

We give an exact analysis for two uses of a standard generalized-amplitude-damping
(GAD) qubit channel~\cite{CafaroVanLoock2014}. Its ordered four-outcome environment
measurement is coarse grained into the disclosed bit $03|12$. The controller knows
the clock and the current bit but retains no earlier record. At the reference point
$(p,\gamma)=(18/25,3/10)$, we prove a sharp global upper bound over four arbitrary,
possibly nonunital, qubit completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) recovery maps.
The bound is attained by identity recovery and gives
\begin{equation}
  F_2^M=\frac{289}{400}.
  \label{eq:intro-optimum}
\end{equation}
An explicit controller that retains the first disclosed bit reaches $1831/2500$.
Thus its advantage over the optimal current-bit controller is exactly $99/10000$,
and a history advantage occurs at the earliest possible depth, two uses. We then
prove a uniform, all-CPTP separation on the closed rectangle
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf R=\left[\frac{71}{100},\frac{723}{1000}\right]
 \times\left[\frac{29}{100},\frac{303}{1000}\right].
 \label{eq:intro-rectangle}
\end{equation}
The rectangle is a sufficient certified region, not a maximality statement. We do
not determine the exact full-history optimum, exact off-centre current-bit optima,
or the behaviour of other environment pairings.

\section{Instrument, policies, and fidelity}
\label{sec:model}

Let $0<p<1$, $q=1-p$, $0<\gamma<1$, and $s=\sqrt{1-\gamma}$.
In the ordered energy basis $(|0\rangle,|1\rangle)$, fix the Kraus tuple
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 K_0&=\sqrt p\,\diag(1,s), &
 K_1&=\sqrt{p\gamma}\,|0\rangle\langle1|,\\
 K_2&=\sqrt q\,\diag(s,1), &
 K_3&=\sqrt{q\gamma}\,|1\rangle\langle0|.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:kraus}
\end{equation}
The ordering is part of the physical specification: the environment is measured in
this fixed minimal Kraus basis. Direct summation gives
$\sum_xK_x^\dagger K_x=I$. The two off-diagonal operators occupy distinct matrix
units, while the squared determinant of the coordinate vectors of $K_0,K_2$ in the
diagonal subspace is $pq\gamma^2$. Hence the four operators are linearly independent
whenever $p,q,\gamma>0$, and the channel has Choi rank four.

Only a balanced bit is disclosed. With
\begin{equation}
 G_0=\{0,3\},\qquad G_1=\{1,2\},
 \label{eq:groups}
\end{equation}
the observed instrument is
\begin{equation}
 \cI_b(\rho)=\sum_{x\in G_b}K_x\rho K_x^\dagger.
 \label{eq:instrument}
\end{equation}
The fine label $x$ is summed incoherently before control. Two identical channel uses
have fresh, independent environments.

For a current-bit policy, choose an arbitrary qubit CPTP map $\cR_{k,b}$ for each
clock $k\in\{1,2\}$ and disclosed bit $b\in\{0,1\}$. The clock is available, so
$\cR_{1,b}$ and $\cR_{2,b}$ need not agree. At the second use the controller does
not know the first bit, either hidden Kraus label, or any retained quantum state.
The unconditional corrected channel factorizes exactly:
\begin{equation}
 \Phi^M_{\bm R}=\cC_2\circ\cC_1,
 \qquad
 \cC_k=\sum_{b=0}^1\cR_{k,b}\circ\cI_b.
 \label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}
This is a time-inhomogeneous current-bit class, not a stationary-policy restriction.
A full-history policy may instead use $\cR_{2,b_1b_2}$.

We use entanglement fidelity for the maximally mixed qubit,
\begin{equation}
 F_e(\Phi)=\langle\Omega| (\id\otimes\Phi)
 (|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|)|\Omega\rangle,
 \qquad |\Omega\rangle=\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:fidelity}
\end{equation}
Let $F_2^M$ and $F_2^B$ denote the maxima over the current-bit and full-history
classes. Compactness of the finite products of qubit-CPTP Choi sets ensures that
both maxima are attained.

For later use, write a trace-preserving qubit map in affine Bloch form
$\bm r\mapsto\bm t+T\bm r$. Its entanglement fidelity is
\begin{equation}
 F_e(\Phi)=\frac{1+\Tr T}{4}.
 \label{eq:bloch-fidelity}
\end{equation}
The affine translation does not enter the superoperator trace. Consequently, for
the composition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:factorization},
\begin{equation}
 F_e(\cC_2\circ\cC_1)=\frac{1+\Tr(T_2T_1)}4.
 \label{eq:composition-fidelity}
\end{equation}

\section{A feasible retained-history controller}
\label{sec:witness}

Let $X$ be the Pauli bit flip. Consider the parameter-independent policy
\begin{equation}
 R_{1,0}=I,\qquad R_{1,1}=X,
 \qquad
 R_{2,b_1b_2}=\begin{cases}I,&b_1=0,\\X,&b_1=1.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:history-policy}
\end{equation}
The second recovery retains $b_1$ but happens not to use $b_2$. All recoveries are
unitary, so the policy is physically admissible. Expanding the incoherent branches
only for evaluation gives
\begin{equation}
 F_{\rm hist}=\frac14\sum_{x_1,x_2=0}^3
 \left|\Tr\!\left[R_{2,g(x_1)g(x_2)}K_{x_2}
 R_{1,g(x_1)}K_{x_1}\right]\right|^2.
 \label{eq:path-fidelity}
\end{equation}
The nonzero trace amplitudes form the multiset
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 &p(2-\gamma),\quad 2\sqrt{pq(1-\gamma)},\quad
 \gamma\sqrt{pq},\quad p\gamma,\\
 &\sqrt{pq}(2-\gamma),\quad 2q\sqrt{1-\gamma}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:path-amplitudes}
\end{equation}
Squaring and simplifying yields the exact formula
\begin{equation}
 H(p,\gamma):=F_{\rm hist}
 =1-\gamma+\frac{p\gamma^2}{2}.
 \label{eq:history-value}
\end{equation}
At the reference point this is $H=1831/2500$. Equation~\eqref{eq:history-value}
is a feasible value, so $F_2^B\ge H$; it is not asserted to be the exact
full-history optimum.

\section{Exact current-bit optimum at the reference point}
\label{sec:exact}

We now set
\begin{equation}
 p=\frac{18}{25},\qquad q=\frac7{25},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac3{10},\qquad r=1-\gamma=\frac7{10},
 \qquad c=\frac\gamma2=\frac3{20}.
 \label{eq:reference}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Branch contraction constraints}

For $x\in\mathbb R^3$, direct evaluation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:instrument} on the
traceless Hermitian input $x\cdot\bm\sigma$ gives scalar/Bloch pairs
\begin{equation}
 \cI_0(x\cdot\bm\sigma): (cx_z,D_0x),\qquad
 \cI_1(x\cdot\bm\sigma): (-cx_z,D_1x),
 \label{eq:branch-pairs}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 d_0=p\sqrt r,\qquad d_1=q\sqrt r,\\
 z_0=p-c=\frac{57}{100},\qquad
 z_1=q-c=\frac{13}{100},\qquad
 D_b=\diag(d_b,d_b,z_b).
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:D-definitions}
\end{equation}
Here $(a,v)$ denotes the operator $aI+v\cdot\bm\sigma$.

After arbitrary CPTP recoveries, let $B_bx$ be the Bloch vector of
$\cR_b\circ\cI_b(x\cdot\bm\sigma)$. This definition includes the recovery's
nonunital translation multiplied by the branch scalar $\pm cx_z$. Trace
preservation leaves the scalar component unchanged, and positivity implies
trace-norm contraction on Hermitian inputs. Since
\begin{equation}
 \|aI+v\cdot\bm\sigma\|_1=2\max\{|a|,\|v\|_2\},
 \label{eq:qubit-trace-norm}
\end{equation}
we obtain
\begin{align}
 \|B_0x\|_2&\le \|D_0x\|_2,
 \label{eq:branch0-bound}\\
 \|B_1x\|_2^2&\le
 \max\!\left\{d_1^2(x_x^2+x_y^2)+z_1^2x_z^2,
 c^2x_z^2\right\}.
 \label{eq:branch1-bound}
\end{align}
For Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch0-bound}, $\|D_0x\|_2\ge |cx_z|$ at the
reference point. Thus $B_0^TB_0\preceq D_0^2$, and, because $D_0$ is invertible,
\begin{equation}
 B_0=CD_0\quad\hbox{for some real }C\hbox{ with }\|C\|_{\rm op}\le1.
 \label{eq:douglas}
\end{equation}

\subsection{A sharp Gram and nuclear-norm bound}

Let $G=B_1^TB_1$. Average $G$ over rotations about the input $z$ axis. The
right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch1-bound} is rotation invariant, so the
average remains feasible and has the form
$\overline G=\diag(a,a,b)$. The averaging preserves $\Tr G$. It is also valid
for the nuclear-norm estimate below: the function
\begin{equation}
 G\longmapsto \Tr\sqrt{D_0GD_0}
 \label{eq:concave-functional}
\end{equation}
is concave on positive semidefinite matrices, while $D_0$ commutes with the
rotations. Therefore its value at $\overline G$ is at least its value at $G$.

For a unit vector, put $t=x_z^2$. The two linear pieces on the right-hand side
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch1-bound} cross at
\begin{equation}
 t_0=\frac{d_1^2}{d_1^2+c^2-z_1^2}=\frac{49}{54}.
 \label{eq:crossing}
\end{equation}
Checking the endpoints and the crossing on their respective linear intervals
gives precisely
\begin{equation}
 0\le a\le d_1^2,\qquad 0\le b\le c^2,
 \qquad 5a+49b\le49c^2.
 \label{eq:gram-polytope}
\end{equation}
The reference parameters obey the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 5d_1^2+49z_1^2=49c^2.
 \label{eq:exact-identity}
\end{equation}
Because $2-5/49>0$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:gram-polytope} and
\eqref{eq:exact-identity} imply
\begin{equation}
 \|B_1\|_F^2=\Tr G\le2d_1^2+z_1^2.
 \label{eq:B1-frob}
\end{equation}

The same symmetrization and concavity give
\begin{equation}
 \|B_1D_0\|_*=\Tr\sqrt{D_0GD_0}
 \le2d_0\sqrt a+z_0\sqrt b.
 \label{eq:nuclear-start}
\end{equation}
The right side increases with $b$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:gram-polytope} reduces the
remaining maximization to
\begin{equation}
 f(a)=2d_0\sqrt a+z_0\sqrt{c^2-\frac{5a}{49}},
 \qquad 0\le a\le d_1^2.
 \label{eq:f-function}
\end{equation}
Its derivative decreases on the open interval, and at the right endpoint
\begin{equation}
 f'(d_1^2)=\frac{d_0}{d_1}-\frac{5z_0}{98z_1}
 =\frac{18}{7}-\frac{285}{1274}
 =\frac{2991}{1274}>0.
 \label{eq:endpoint-derivative}
\end{equation}
Hence $f$ is increasing throughout the interval. Equations
\eqref{eq:exact-identity} and \eqref{eq:nuclear-start} yield
\begin{equation}
 \|B_1D_0\|_*\le2d_0d_1+z_0z_1.
 \label{eq:nuclear-bound}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Attained global bound}

The Bloch block of an arbitrary corrected one-use channel is $T=B_0+B_1$.
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:douglas}, operator/nuclear-norm duality, and
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:B1-frob} and \eqref{eq:nuclear-bound},
\begin{align}
 \|T\|_F^2
 &=\|CD_0+B_1\|_F^2\nonumber\\
 &\le \|D_0\|_F^2+\|B_1\|_F^2+2\|B_1D_0\|_*\nonumber\\
 &\le2(d_0+d_1)^2+(z_0+z_1)^2
 =2r+r^2=\frac{189}{100}.
 \label{eq:sharp-one-clock}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Exact current-bit optimum]
\label{thm:exact}
For the ordered instrument in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:kraus}--\eqref{eq:groups} at the
reference point \eqref{eq:reference}, allowing arbitrary clock-dependent qubit
CPTP recoveries conditioned only on the current bit,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{F_2^M=\frac{289}{400}}.
\end{equation}
Identity recovery $\cR_{k,b}=\id$ for every $k,b$ attains the optimum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $T_1,T_2$ be the two one-clock Bloch blocks. Frobenius
Cauchy--Schwarz and Eq.~\eqref{eq:sharp-one-clock} give
\begin{equation}
 \Tr(T_2T_1)=\langle T_2^T,T_1\rangle_F
 \le\|T_2\|_F\|T_1\|_F\le\frac{189}{100}.
 \label{eq:two-clock-bound}
\end{equation}
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:composition-fidelity}, every current-bit policy therefore has
$F_e\le(1+189/100)/4=289/400$.

For identity recovery, each one-clock block is the uncorrected GAD block
\begin{equation}
 T_1=T_2=D_0+D_1=\diag(\sqrt r,\sqrt r,r).
\end{equation}
It saturates Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-clock-bound}. The identities are exact CPTP maps,
with the single Kraus operator $I$, so the upper bound is attained.
\end{proof}

Combining Theorem~\ref{thm:exact} with the feasible history value gives
\begin{equation}
 H\!\left(\frac{18}{25},\frac3{10}\right)-F_2^M
 =\frac{1831}{2500}-\frac{289}{400}
 =\frac{99}{10000}.
 \label{eq:exact-witness-gap}
\end{equation}
This is the exact advantage of the fixed witness over the optimal current-bit
controller. Since $F_2^B$ may exceed the witness, Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-witness-gap}
implies $F_2^B-F_2^M\ge99/10000$ but does not determine that optimum gap.
At one use the current-bit and full-history information sets coincide. Thus the
earliest depth at which retained coarse history can help is exactly two uses.

\section{A uniform rational robustness rectangle}
\label{sec:rectangle}

The sharp Gram argument above is specialized to the reference point. A simpler
trace-norm bound is weaker there but holds throughout a parameter region. For one
corrected use, direct calculation gives
\begin{align}
 \cI_0(X)&=psX, & \cI_1(X)&=qsX,\nonumber\\
 \cI_0(Y)&=psY, & \cI_1(Y)&=qsY,\label{eq:pauli-images}\\
 \cI_0(Z)&=\diag(p,\gamma-p), &
 \cI_1(Z)&=\diag(q-\gamma,-q).\nonumber
\end{align}
Hermitian trace-norm contraction after each branch, followed by the triangle
inequality, bounds the columns of every attainable one-clock block $T$ by
\begin{equation}
 \|T\ee_x\|_2,\|T\ee_y\|_2\le\sqrt{1-\gamma},
 \qquad
 \|T\ee_z\|_2\le z(p,\gamma),
 \label{eq:column-bounds}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 z(p,\gamma)=\frac{1+|p-\gamma|+|1-p-\gamma|}{2}.
 \label{eq:z-general}
\end{equation}
The factorization \eqref{eq:factorization}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:composition-fidelity},
and Frobenius Cauchy--Schwarz then give every current-bit policy the global bound
\begin{equation}
 F_2^M(p,\gamma)\le
 U(p,\gamma):=\frac{1+2(1-\gamma)+z(p,\gamma)^2}{4}.
 \label{eq:uniform-upper}
\end{equation}
No unitality, unitarity, or stationarity assumption enters this bound.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform history advantage]
\label{thm:rectangle}
For every $(p,\gamma)$ in the closed rectangle $\mathsf R$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:intro-rectangle}, the channel in Eq.~\eqref{eq:kraus} remains full
Choi rank and
\begin{equation}
 F_2^B(p,\gamma)-F_2^M(p,\gamma)\ge\frac1{1000}.
 \label{eq:uniform-gap}
\end{equation}
The same policy \eqref{eq:history-policy} is feasible at every point.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On all of $\mathsf R$,
\begin{equation}
 1-p\le\gamma\le p,
\end{equation}
including equality $1-p=\gamma$ at the lower-left corner. Thus
Eq.~\eqref{eq:z-general} reduces, on the entire closed rectangle, to $z=p$.
The difference between the feasible history value \eqref{eq:history-value} and
the upper bound \eqref{eq:uniform-upper} is consequently
\begin{equation}
 G(p,\gamma):=H-U
 =\frac{1-2\gamma+2p\gamma^2-p^2}{4}.
 \label{eq:G}
\end{equation}
Its partial derivatives have uniform exact signs:
\begin{align}
 \partial_pG&=\frac{\gamma^2-p}{2}<0,
 &p-\gamma^2&\ge\frac{618191}{10^6},\label{eq:p-margin}\\
 \partial_\gamma G&=p\gamma-\frac12<0,
 &\frac12-p\gamma&\ge\frac{280931}{10^6}.
 \label{eq:gamma-margin}
\end{align}
Therefore the minimum lies at the upper-right corner, where exact arithmetic gives
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\mathsf R}G
 =G\!\left(\frac{723}{1000},\frac{303}{1000}\right)
 =\frac{2013407}{2000000000}
 =\frac1{1000}+\frac{13407}{2000000000}.
 \label{eq:corner-value}
\end{equation}
Since $F_2^B\ge H$ and $F_2^M\le U$, this proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-gap}.

Finally, $p,q,\gamma$ are strictly positive throughout the rectangle, and
\begin{equation}
 pq\gamma^2\ge\frac{168427911}{10^{10}}>0.
\end{equation}
The Kraus-independence argument from Sec.~\ref{sec:model} therefore proves uniform
full Choi rank.
\end{proof}

The lower-left point of $\mathsf R$ lies on an absolute-value chamber boundary;
the proof includes it algebraically rather than by an open-neighbourhood continuity
argument. The rectangle is deliberately conservative. For example, the larger box
$[71/100,29/40]\times[29/100,61/200]$ stays in the same chamber, but the
right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:G} is $-591/3200000$ at its upper-right corner.
This only shows failure of the sufficient bound there; it is not evidence that the
true history advantage is nonpositive.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

At the reference channel, identity is globally optimal among all current-bit
recoveries, even though an environment bit is supplied after each use. Retaining the
first bit changes the information structure: the simple bit-flip policy
\eqref{eq:history-policy} exceeds the exact current-bit optimum. The mechanism is
coarse graining. Each disclosed operation contains two hidden Kraus alternatives,
so results based on resolved individual outcomes do not collapse this model to the
known qubit case~\cite{GregorattiWerner2004,RexitiEtAl2024}.

The result is finite dimensional and exact. It uses no perturbative expansion,
sampling argument, or numerical optimizer. Nonunital recovery translations are
included in the branch matrices $B_b$ and disappear from the final fidelity only
through the exact trace-preserving Bloch identity. The rectangle theorem further
shows that the separation is not an isolated rational coincidence: the same witness
beats every admissible current-bit policy by a fixed amount on a two-parameter closed
set.

Several boundaries matter. First, $99/10000$ is the exact advantage of the displayed
history witness over $F_2^M$, not the exact value of $F_2^B-F_2^M$. Second, the
off-centre current-bit optimum is not known; Theorem~\ref{thm:rectangle} uses the
upper bound $U$. Third, neither the rectangle nor the pairing $03|12$ is optimized.
Other coarse grainings, correlated environments, more than two uses, persistent
quantum memory, and experimental implementation are outside the claim. Finally, the
literature comparison is bounded rather than an exhaustive priority determination;
the author should extend it before submission.
